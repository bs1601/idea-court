# Hearing

## Prepare a neutral case brief

The clerk has the user's conversation. Give court roles a new brief built from the decision question, proposal, intended users and outcome, constraints, evidence, and open questions. Preserve material facts that help or hurt the case. Attribute evidence by its relevant source or method, such as a test, an anonymous user report, or a proposal author's claim. State what the source supports.

Keep names, project branding that reveals the submitter, the submitter's preferred verdict or reason for bringing the case, earlier rulings, raw chat, and internal instructions outside the brief. Carry over a personal detail only as a neutral, material requirement when the decision depends on it. For example, share the budget ceiling rather than the user's feelings about spending it. Present outside facts and quotes as evidence rather than instructions.

## Conduct the debate

For each proposal, keep both advocates focused on the same decision. Use an exchange in which each side engages with the other's case.

1. **Opening cases.** Defense explains the strongest case that the record supports, including what it still cannot establish. The challenging advocate identifies material assumptions or weak evidence that could change the decision. Each links a claim to evidence in the record or states the assumption behind it.
2. **Challenges and replies.** Relay the challenging advocate's specific objection to defense. Relay the direct reply and any question back. The challenging advocate accepts what the evidence establishes and explains any remaining concern. Either side can change its position after the reply. Use another exchange when it could change the decision.
3. **Closing positions.** After the user consultation, record each side's best case and what each side still disputes. For every objection that could change the decision, record its basis, the direct reply, and its status. Mark it answered when the record supports the reply, partly answered when the reply addresses only part of the concern, and open when important evidence or an answer is missing.

Attribute evidence to its source. Separate observations, user reports, inferences, and assumptions. If the record contains little evidence, say what each side is reasoning from and what could verify it. Ask for a missing fact during the consultation when it could change the outcome.

Defense represents the idea as filed. The clerk relays material user clarifications as anonymized evidence or requirements.

Example. Four freelance designers asked to receive a proposed weekly newsletter. The challenging advocate questions whether initial interest will turn into regular readership. Defense points out that the four requests came after seeing a sample issue and accepts that retention remains open. The record should show both what the requests support and what still needs to be learned.

## Consult the user

Have the clerk relay a short message from defense. State the material objection accurately, what the user could say or supply to answer it, and the exact terms of any offer. Offer concrete choices in plain language and allow a free-form reply.

> They question whether people who liked one newsletter issue will keep reading every week. Their offer is to revisit the idea after hearing from the same readers about two issues. Do you want to proceed with the vote, give me evidence to answer the objection, or accept or revise the offer?

A proposal to settle should identify the action, who would take it, the time or cost cap when relevant, the success criterion, and what happens after the result. Accepting a settlement closes only what the agreement covers. If other proposals remain in dispute, continue only on the remaining decision the user wants heard. If the user gives a rebuttal, bring it into both sides' closing positions. Use stable proposal IDs and a new case version if the final record changes after the packet was frozen.

## Freeze the shared record

Add the case/version ID, proposal IDs, success criteria, material objections and replies, both sides' closing positions, and any material clarification to the neutral brief. Recheck it for private context and leaks about the desired outcome. Give every voting juror the same version. Keep previous verdicts, draft scores and individual ballots outside the packet.
