# Pilot cases

Use these cases to check the workflow after installing the skill. Prefix each prompt with `$idea-court` in Codex or `/idea-court` in Claude Code.

## Put an idea on trial

> I'm considering a browser extension that saves quotes from articles into a research notebook. I have one week for validation and four prospective users who agreed to test. Should I spend that week building a small working prototype? Give me a chance to respond before the jury votes.

When the advocate consults you, try supplying this evidence.

> Two testers told me that their existing bookmark tool saves the article but loses the exact paragraph. I'd like to keep the one-week scope. Please proceed with the vote.

Look for both sides to address what that evidence changes. The judge should report a single decision with 12 valid ballots and a concrete next step.

## Bring a development problem

> Five of eight trial users stopped during onboarding. Today we ask them to connect three accounts before they can see their first report. We can spend one sprint testing an improvement and we must still support all three connections eventually. I'm open to different approaches. Have the board propose solutions and run a hearing.

Try either branch in a separate session.

- Ask the advocate to negotiate a narrower test, then accept its exact terms. Look for an agreed settlement with its action and success criterion.
- Ask to proceed with the alternatives. Look for each admitted proposal to receive its own tally from the same panel and an explanation of what to test first.

If the host cannot launch isolated jurors, the correct result for the voting branch is a pending vote. When a jury runs, each seat should receive only the same closed record and its own role, with no earlier ballots.

## Compare with a short critique

Run the same case and evidence in two fresh sessions. In one session use Idea Court. In the other ask for the strongest reason for, the strongest reason against, and the smallest experiment that would change the recommendation. Compare the time to a decision, which material assumptions each response surfaced, and whether either response changed your intended next action.
